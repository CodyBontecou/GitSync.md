Obsidian iCloud sync: setup and fixes.

One checklist for putting an Obsidian vault in iCloud on iPhone, iPad and Mac, and for working out why it stopped syncing, is duplicating files or is missing on another device.

By Cody Bontecou November 23, 2026 Guide iPhone + iPad + Mac

Sources

Setup steps and warnings follow Obsidian Help: Sync your notes across devices (checked 2026-11-23). This guide does not describe iCloud internals beyond what that page and Apple's support pages say.

Short answer

Obsidian works with iCloud on iPhone, iPad and Mac when the vault sits in the Obsidian folder inside iCloud Drive (iCloud Drive/Obsidian/Your Vault) and its files are kept downloaded. On Windows or Android, use something else: Obsidian warns that iCloud Drive on Windows may lead to file duplication or corruption, and this guide covers Apple devices only. Choosing between methods? See iCloud vs Git for Obsidian.

Before you start

  • iCloud Drive is on. On a Mac, open System Settings, choose your name, then iCloud, then Drive (Apple Support: iCloud Drive on Mac). On iPhone and iPad, the Settings path is Settings → your name → iCloud → Drive; you can then see the files in the Files app under Browse → Locations → iCloud Drive.
  • The same Apple Account on every device. Files only travel between devices signed in to the account that owns the iCloud storage.
  • Enough iCloud storage. The vault uses your iCloud storage quota, attachments included.

Set up a new iCloud vault

The rule that matters most, from Obsidian Help: Sync your notes across devices: vaults should be inside the Obsidian folder within iCloud Drive. That is the folder created by Obsidian, not a plain folder you made yourself with a similar name.

On iPhone or iPad

  1. Open Obsidian and choose Create new vault.
  2. Name the vault.
  3. Turn on Store in iCloud.
  4. Tap Create.

On a Mac

  1. Choose Create new vault and name it.
  2. Set the location to iCloud Drive/Obsidian, so the vault is iCloud Drive/Obsidian/Your Vault.

On a second Apple device

  1. Open the vault switcher and choose Open folder as vault.
  2. Browse to iCloud Drive → Obsidian and pick the vault.

Keep the files downloaded

Obsidian can only read notes that are on the device. Per Obsidian's help:

  • macOS 15 or later (and iOS/iPadOS 18 or later): right-click the Obsidian folder in iCloud Drive and choose Keep Downloaded.
  • macOS 14 and earlier: turn off Optimize Mac Storage in iCloud settings. This setting applies to all of iCloud Drive on that Mac, not only Obsidian.

Move an existing vault into iCloud

  1. Make a copy first. A separate backup gives you a way back; see how to back up an Obsidian vault with Git.
  2. Close Obsidian on every device that has the vault open.
  3. Move the vault folder into iCloud Drive/Obsidian with Finder or the Files app.
  4. Wait for the upload to finish on the first device before opening the vault anywhere else.
  5. On each other device, use Open folder as vault and pick it under iCloud Drive → Obsidian.

iCloud sync not working: symptom, cause, fix

Check these first: Is the vault inside iCloud Drive/Obsidian? Does the Files app show a cloud or download icon next to notes? Is Obsidian Sync or another plugin or service also syncing this vault? Are all devices signed in to the same Apple Account with iCloud Drive on, with iCloud storage free?

Duplicate and conflicted files

A note named like Meeting 2.md next to Meeting.md is a second copy of the same note. It usually means two versions existed at once, for example after editing on two devices before they synced. Obsidian's help warns that iCloud Drive on Windows can duplicate files and that mixing sync services on one vault should be avoided, and both make this worse.

  1. Close Obsidian on every device so nothing writes while you work.
  2. Open both files side by side in a text editor and compare them.
  3. Copy any lines you want to keep into the file you will retain.
  4. Delete the extra copy, then wait for the change to sync before reopening Obsidian elsewhere.

When iCloud is the wrong tool

  • Windows: Obsidian's help says iCloud Drive on Windows may lead to file duplication or corruption.
  • Android: this guide covers Apple devices, and iCloud Drive isn't how Obsidian syncs on Android. See Obsidian Git on Android.
  • History you can restore: Git records the commits you make.

Git is the alternative. The vault lives in a GitHub or GitLab repository; on iPhone and iPad, GitSync.md clones it into a local folder, usually On My iPhone/iPad → Obsidian, which is not iCloud, and Obsidian opens it with Open folder as vault. The full steps are in Obsidian Git on iPhone and iPad; for a Windows and iPhone pair, see Sync Obsidian between Windows and iPhone with GitHub. Android users can get GitSync.md on Google Play. Other options are in Obsidian Sync alternatives and Obsidian and Google Drive.

Don't run iCloud and Git on the same active vault. To switch, make a separate copy of the vault, set Git up on that copy, and stop iCloud syncing that copy. Keep the original until the new setup is verified. The background-sync guide explains what to expect from pulls and pushes on iOS.

FAQ

Is Obsidian iCloud sync free?

Obsidian doesn't charge for it, but the vault counts against your iCloud storage. Check your plan if the vault is large.

Can I use iCloud and Obsidian Sync together?

No, not on the same vault. Obsidian's help says to avoid syncing the same vault across multiple services, and gives Obsidian Sync with iCloud as an example.

Does Obsidian iCloud sync work on Windows?

Obsidian's help warns that iCloud Drive on Windows may lead to file duplication or corruption. For a Windows vault, use another method such as Git.

Why is my vault not showing on my iPhone?

Most often it is not inside the Obsidian folder in iCloud Drive, or the other device is not signed in to the same Apple Account with iCloud Drive turned on. Obsidian's help says vaults should be inside the Obsidian folder within iCloud Drive.

Can I keep iCloud for my Mac and use Git for Windows?

Not on the same active vault. Keep iCloud for a vault that lives only on Apple devices, and give any vault you need on Windows its own Git working copy.
