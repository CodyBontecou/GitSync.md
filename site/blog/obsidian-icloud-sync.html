Obsidian iCloud sync: set it up and fix it on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

iCloud Drive can keep one Obsidian vault in step across a Mac, an iPhone and an iPad. Here is the setup Obsidian documents, a fix for each common failure, and when to move to Git.

Short answer

Create the vault in Obsidian with Store in iCloud on, so it sits at iCloud Drive → Obsidian → your vault, and open that same folder on every Apple device. Obsidian Help: Sync your notes across devices lists iCloud as a way to sync vaults between iOS and macOS. Most failures come from a vault outside that folder, files that iCloud hasn’t downloaded, or a second sync service on the same vault.

Works best when every device is an Apple device and you don’t need note history. Skip it when Windows, Linux or Android is in the mix, you want history, or conflicts keep coming back. For that choice, see iCloud vs Git for Obsidian.

Set up an iCloud vault the documented way

These steps follow Obsidian Help: Sync your notes across devices (iCloud section), checked 2026-11-24. First turn on iCloud Drive: on iPhone and iPad under Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud → iCloud Drive, on Mac under System Settings → Apple ID → iCloud → iCloud Drive. Apple’s page Set up iCloud Drive on all your devices covers the same switch.

On iPhone or iPad

  1. In Obsidian, tap Create new vault, enter a name, turn on Store in iCloud, and tap Create.
  2. The vault lives at iCloud Drive → Obsidian → your vault. Obsidian’s help says it must be stored there, and warns not to use the plain folder without the app icon. A vault stored elsewhere may have syncing issues.

On a Mac

  1. Open Obsidian. If the vault was created on the phone, use the vault switcher, choose Open folder as vault and select iCloud Drive → Obsidian → your vault. Don’t create a look-alike folder somewhere else.
  2. To start from the Mac instead, choose Create new vault, and in the file picker go to iCloud Drive → Obsidian and create the vault folder there.

Moving an existing Mac vault into iCloud

Copy the vault folder into iCloud Drive → Obsidian, open the copy as a vault on the Mac, then open the same folder on the phone. Keep the original until the notes have finished uploading and you have checked the phone. Obsidian’s help doesn’t spell out this copy step, so treat it as the same “open that folder” rule applied to an existing vault.

Keep the vault downloaded

Obsidian calls this optional but strongly recommended for fast load times. On macOS 15 or later, right-click the Obsidian folder in iCloud Drive and choose Keep Downloaded. On macOS 14 or earlier, turn off Optimize Mac Storage in iCloud settings; Obsidian notes that this affects all iCloud storage on the device, not just Obsidian.

Fixes, by symptom

The vault doesn’t appear on iPhone

What you see: the vault is missing from Obsidian’s vault list, or from iCloud Drive → Obsidian in Files.

Likely causes: iCloud Drive is off, or the vault wasn’t created in iCloud Drive → Obsidian. Obsidian says a vault stored elsewhere may have syncing issues.

Fix: turn on iCloud Drive under Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud → iCloud Drive. Then, in Obsidian’s vault switcher, choose Open folder as vault and pick iCloud Drive → Obsidian → your vault. If the vault sits elsewhere on the Mac, copy it into iCloud Drive → Obsidian and open that copy.

Notes are missing or stale on the phone

What you see: an old version of a note, or a note that shows on the Mac but not on the phone.

Likely cause: iCloud keeps some files only in the cloud until they are needed. Obsidian’s FAQ says to make sure Keep Downloaded is enabled so iCloud doesn’t offload files Obsidian needs. On a Mac, Apple says Optimize Mac Storage can store documents in iCloud when it needs space.

Fix on iPhone and iPad: in Files, touch and hold the file or folder and tap Keep Downloaded. Apple’s iPhone User Guide says that if the option is missing, the file is already on your iPhone. Fix on Mac: use Keep Downloaded or turn off Optimize Mac Storage, as above.

Duplicate files such as “note 2.md”, or conflicts

What you see: two copies of a note, or a note that lost an edit.

Documented causes: Obsidian says to avoid syncing the same vault across multiple services, such as Obsidian Sync and iCloud at once, because it can cause data conflicts or corruption. Its Obsidian Sync switching guide likewise says it doesn’t recommend using Obsidian Sync alongside cloud storage services. On Windows, see the next fix. I found no Apple or Obsidian page that explains numbered copies for other cases, so I don’t claim a cause for them.

Fix: turn off all but one sync method for that vault. Then open each pair of copies, copy the lines you want into one note, and delete the other. After that, wait for the upload to finish on one device before editing on another.

Slow first sync or a large vault

What you see: a new device shows few notes, or loads slowly.

Likely causes: the files haven’t finished uploading or downloading, or iCloud storage is full. Apple says you get 5 GB of free iCloud storage and that you can buy more or delete data if you need space. Apple’s iCloud+ plans and pricing page lists the paid tiers.

Fix: leave both devices online and Obsidian closed on the new one until the files arrive, use Keep Downloaded for the vault folder, and check that your iCloud storage has room for the whole vault.

Windows is in the mix

Obsidian’s help warns: “iCloud Drive on Windows may lead to file duplication or corruption.” (Obsidian Help.) If you use a Windows PC, don’t point it at an iCloud vault. Pick a method that supports Windows; Obsidian Sync alternatives compares them, and Sync Obsidian between Windows and iPhone with GitHub walks through the Git route.

iCloud has no real version history for notes

Apple’s Recover deleted files on iCloud.com page covers files deleted in the last 30 days, and permanently removed files can’t be recovered. That is deleted-file recovery with a time limit. I found no Apple page that documents a per-note edit history. Git keeps every commit you push, so an old version of a note stays retrievable for as long as the repository exists. See how to back up an Obsidian vault with Git.

When to move to Git instead

  • You use Windows, Linux or Android. Obsidian warns about iCloud Drive on Windows, and its iCloud section covers iOS and macOS only.
  • You want history you can browse and restore from.
  • Conflicts and duplicates keep coming back.

GitSync.md clones into a local Files location that Obsidian can open, such as On My iPhone → Obsidian, and pulls and pushes over HTTPS or SSH to GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Gitea or Forgejo. Background Sync is optional and best effort, because iOS decides when apps run.

  1. Make a separate local copy of the vault outside iCloud: in Obsidian create a new vault under On My iPhone → Obsidian with Store in iCloud off, and copy your notes in.
  2. Follow Sync Obsidian with Git on iPhone and iPad for the clone, authentication and first sync.
  3. Retire the iCloud copy only after the Git copy opens correctly on every device.

Never put a Git working copy inside iCloud Drive. Two systems would rewrite the same folder, and Obsidian advises against syncing one vault through multiple services. FAQ

Is iCloud sync for Obsidian free?

Apple says you get 5 GB of free iCloud storage when you sign in and turn on iCloud, and you can buy more. A vault stored in iCloud Drive counts against that storage. Obsidian’s sync help lists iCloud as a way to sync vaults between iOS and macOS.

Does Obsidian iCloud sync work with Android?

Obsidian’s sync help describes iCloud as a way to sync vaults between iOS and macOS, and lists no Android route for it. For Android, use Obsidian Sync or another method, such as Git with GitSync.md.

Can I use iCloud and Git together on the same folder?

No, not on the same folder. Obsidian advises against syncing one vault across multiple services, and a Git working copy inside iCloud Drive means two systems rewrite the same files. Keep the Git copy in a local folder outside iCloud.

Why does Obsidian Sync cost money if iCloud is free?

They are different products, and this page can’t speak to Obsidian’s pricing decisions. Obsidian Sync is a paid Obsidian service that works across platforms, including Windows and Android, while iCloud is limited to Apple devices and is covered by your Apple storage plan.
